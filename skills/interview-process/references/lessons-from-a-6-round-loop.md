# Lessons from a 6-round loop (the source of this process)

A senior infrastructure-engineering loop: six rounds over about five weeks, ~6.5 hours of interviews.

## Stages

1. Research & application
2. Recruiter screen
3. Hiring manager
4. Take-home coding challenge
5. Technical panel × 3 (one interviewer swapped in last minute)
6. Final with an engineering VP
7. Follow-up emails, then the role was put on hold

## Artifacts that earned their keep

| Artifact | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Pre-brief per interviewer | Their background + targeted questions |
| Same-day review | Captured while still fresh |
| Fair review after cool-down | Corrected the same-day mood swing |
| Cross-panel retrospective | Showed patterns no single round showed |
| `status.md` | Timeline, probability, email drafts in one place |
| One hero story | A personal story about trying the company's product that worked in every round |

## Hard lessons (turned into rules)

1. **Use the pedigree questions.** Two interviewers had public work that the prep covered — and
   neither question got asked. Plan them before minute 20.
2. **Pre-empt take-home weaknesses.** A weakness flagged before the panel went unaddressed, and an
   interviewer dug it out live.
3. **Absorb coaching.** When the hiring manager explained the level's bar, the next round
   still got vague answers. Apply what you're taught in the next round.
4. **Recruiter screen motivation: mission first, money later.**
5. **One authentic story beats generic STAR spam.**
6. **Map the room:** advocate vs skeptic vs validator. Talk to each role
   differently.
